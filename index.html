About the workshop

Conversation is one of the most intuitive ways people communicate, and dialogue has long been central to human-robot interaction. Large language models now let robots respond to almost anything a user says, but they bring new problems: unpredictability, latency, shallow or repetitive exchanges, and difficulty grounding talk in multimodal, embodied context.

They also change how we study conversation. How do we run controlled, reproducible studies with systems that can talk about anything, and how can we use them to study genuinely autonomous, open-ended human-robot conversation?

This half-day workshop brings together technical and social perspectives on human-robot dialogue to discuss what the next generation of conversational robots should look like.

Call for papers

We invite contributions on the technical and social sides of human-robot dialogue, from building conversational systems to designing studies that evaluate them.

Topics include, but are not limited to

  • Dialogue system architectures for robots that build on or go beyond LLMs
  • Multimodal input and output: gaze, gesture, prosody, turn-taking
  • Latency, incrementality and real-time conversation
  • Grounding dialogue in embodiment, tasks and the physical environment
  • Long-term, social and relational conversation
  • Study design for open-ended, LLM-driven interactions
  • Reproducibility, control and comparability in dialogue studies
  • Ethical considerations of deploying LLMs in social robots

Submission types

  • Extended abstracts — 2 pages
  • Short papers — 4 pages

Both formats may include up to 2 additional pages for references. Submissions are made through OpenReview , where all papers are peer reviewed.
